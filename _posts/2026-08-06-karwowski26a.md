title: Likelihood hacking in probabilistic program synthesis
abstract: When language models are trained by reinforcement learning ({RL}) to write
  probabilistic programs, they can artificially inflate their marginal-likelihood
  reward by producing programs whose data distribution fails to normalise instead
  of fitting the data better. We call this failure likelihood hacking (LH). We formalise
  LH in a core probabilistic programming language (PPL) and give sufficient syntactic
  conditions for its prevention, proving that a safe language fragment $L_{safe}$
  satisfying these conditions cannot produce likelihood-hacking programs. Empirically,
  we show that GRPO-trained models generating PyMC code discover LH exploits within
  the first few training steps, driving violation rates well above the untrained-model
  baseline. We implement $L_{safe}$’s conditions as SafeStan, a LH-resistant modification
  of Stan, and show empirically that it suppresses LH under optimisation pressure.
  These results show that language-level safety constraints are both theoretically
  grounded and effective in practice for automated {Bayesian} model discovery.
