title: On the Granularity of Causal Effect Identifiability
abstract: 'The classical notion of causal effect identifiability is defined in terms
  of treatment and outcome variables. In this paper, we consider the identifiability
  of state-based causal effects: how an intervention on a particular _state_ of treatment
  variables affects a particular _state_ of outcome variables. We demonstrate that
  state-based causal effects may be identifiable even when variable-based causal effects
  may not. Moreover, we show that this separation occurs only when additional knowledge
  — such as context-specific independencies — is available. We further examine knowledge
  that constrains the states of variables, and show that such knowledge can improve
  both variable-based and state-based identifiability when combined with other knowledge
  such as context-specific independencies. We finally propose an approach for identifying
  causal effects under these additional constraints, and conduct empirical studies
  to further illustrate the separations between the two levels of identifiability.'
