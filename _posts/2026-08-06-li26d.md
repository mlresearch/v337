title: Matched-Pair Experimental Design with Active Learning
abstract: Matched-pair experimental designs aim to detect treatment effects by pairing
  participants and comparing within-pair outcome differences. In many situations,
  the overall effect size across the entire population is small. Then, the focus naturally
  shifts to identifying and targeting high treatment-effect regions where the intervention
  is most effective. This paper proposes a matched-pair experimental design that sequentially
  and actively enrolls patients in high treatment-effect regions. Importantly, we
  frame the identification of the target region as a classification problem and propose
  an active learning framework tailored to matched-pair designs. Our design not only
  reduces the experimental cost of detecting treatment efficacy, but also ensures
  that the identified regions enclose the entire high-treatment-effect regions. Our
  theoretical analysis of the framework’s label complexity and experiments in practical
  scenarios demonstrate the efficiency and advantages of the approach.
