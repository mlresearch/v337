title: Verbalizing LLM’s Higher-order Uncertainty via Imprecise Probabilities
abstract: Despite the growing demand for eliciting uncertainty from large language
  models ({LLMs}), empirical evidence suggests that {LLM} behavior is not always adequately
  captured by the elicitation techniques developed under the classical probabilistic
  uncertainty framework. This mismatch leads to systematic failure modes, particularly
  in settings that involve ambiguous question-answering, in-context learning, and
  self-reflection. To address this, we propose novel prompt-based uncertainty elicitation
  techniques grounded in *imprecise probabilities*, a principled framework for representing
  and eliciting higher-order uncertainty. Here, first-order uncertainty captures uncertainty
  over possible responses to a prompt, while second-order uncertainty (uncertainty
  about uncertainty) quantifies indeterminacy in the underlying probability model
  itself. We introduce general-purpose prompting and post-processing procedures to
  directly elicit and quantify both orders of uncertainty, and demonstrate their effectiveness
  across diverse settings. Our approach enables more faithful uncertainty reporting
  from {LLMs}, improving credibility and supporting downstream decision-making.
