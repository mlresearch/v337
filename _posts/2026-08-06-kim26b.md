title: 'Beyond Bounds: Quantifying the Probability of Counterfactual Fairness'
abstract: Counterfactual fairness is a rigorous criterion for algorithmic decision-making
  but remains fundamentally unidentifiable from observational data alone. Existing
  partial identification methods address this by deriving bounds for fairness measures;
  however, these intervals are often wide, limiting their practical utility. To address
  this limitation, we propose a framework that quantifies the probability that a black-box
  model satisfies counterfactual fairness, under a stated prior over the structural
  causal models compatible with the observed data. By exploiting conditional independencies
  to reduce the parameter space, we demonstrate that the set of causal parameters
  compatible with the observed data forms a convex polytope. We further show that,
  given domain-specific priors on exogenous distributions, this prior-dependent probability
  can be estimated via Hit-and-Run Monte Carlo integration. Our approach provides
  a practical tool that complements worst-case bounds, offering a prior-dependent
  probabilistic summary for auditing model fairness.
